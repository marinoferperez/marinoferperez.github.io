Learning to guide the search

An undergraduate research project on the use of machine learning to hybridise metaheuristics.

2026Research · case study in preparation

Overview

Can a learned model help an optimisation algorithm decide where to search next? This research examines the relationship between machine learning models and the changing populations of evolutionary algorithms.

The undergraduate thesis was submitted at the University of Granada in June 2026. This is a short introduction to the work; the full case study and supporting materials are being prepared.

Problem

An evolutionary algorithm generates data as it searches. But that data is not stationary: the population moves, its diversity changes, and the distribution a model learns from may no longer resemble the next stage of the search.

The central question is whether predictive quality persists across successive stages of optimisation.

Research / approach

The study considers a steady-state genetic algorithm and differential evolution. Models are trained on observations from one part of the optimisation run and evaluated on the following part, preserving temporal order.

Dataset

The experimental setting uses the CEC2017 optimisation benchmark in 10 dimensions. Training examples come from the populations generated during the search, rather than from an independent, fixed dataset.

Methodology

Runs are divided into five blocks, each representing 20% of the evaluation budget. The protocol compares training on the immediately preceding block with accumulating observations from earlier blocks.

Candidate models include LASSO, multilayer perceptrons, support vector regression, random forests, radial basis functions, response surface models, and histogram gradient boosting.

Experiments

Evaluation considers MAE, RMSE, R², Spearman rank correlation, and computation time. Predicting an objective value and preserving the ordering of candidate solutions are treated as different questions.

Results

Verified plots and numerical comparisons will be added with the full case study. No performance improvement is claimed in this preview.

Engineering / production

This is a research study. Reproducibility materials, environment details, and the code repository will be linked when prepared for publication.

Limitations

Results on a fixed benchmark and dimensionality cannot establish general performance across optimisation problems. Changes in population diversity also complicate the interpretation of late-stage predictive metrics.

What I learned

A useful research question goes beyond choosing a model: it also asks when the model is evaluated, which data it sees, and whether the metric reflects the decision the algorithm needs to make.
